## The goal

**By 1 September 2026: a product manager role at a mid-size software company, or a freelance
practice that clears £4,000/month without weekend work.**

Whichever arrives first. Not both, and not "see how it goes."

## Lanes, in priority order

When two lanes want the same hour, the higher one wins. This order is the whole point of the
list — do not reorder it casually.

| # | Lane | What it is | How I know it's working |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Client work** | Technical writing for three retained clients. Pays for everything else. | Invoices out on time, no client waiting more than 48h |
| 2 | **PM transition** | Applications, interviews, the portfolio, conversations with people already doing it | Applications sent, conversations had |
| 3 | **The newsletter** | *Plainly Put* — writing about documentation and product. The thing that makes the PM case for me | Published Thursdays |
| 4 | **Family** | Not a lane I optimise. A lane I protect. | See non-negotiables |

**Hard separation:** client work and the newsletter never reference each other. Clients are not
newsletter material, and no client is ever named in a post. This has come up twice and the
answer is the same both times.

## Day shape

Contiguous. Every hour belongs to a block — if a gap appears here, the brief's dial breaks.

| Block | Hours |
|---|---|
| Sleep | 23:00 – 06:30 |
| Morning / school run | 06:30 – 09:00 |
| Deep work (lane 1 or 2) | 09:00 – 12:30 |
| Lunch and walk | 12:30 – 13:30 |
| Client work | 13:30 – 16:00 |
| School pickup and family | 16:00 – 19:30 |
| Admin / applications / newsletter | 19:30 – 22:00 |
| Wind down | 22:00 – 23:00 |

---

## Non-negotiables

Cut scope to the floor before cutting any of these. On a wrecked day these are what survives.

- **School run at 08:20 and pickup at 16:15.** Not moveable. Not negotiable. Not "usually."
- **Sleep 23:00–06:30.** A plan that needs me past midnight is a bad plan, not a hard day.
- **Thursday evening is Ada's swimming.** 18:00–19:30, in the diary, I am there.
- **Sundays are off.** No client work, no applications, no newsletter. If a week needs Sunday,
  the week was planned wrong.

## Rules I've had to write down

1. **Reply first.** Anything waiting on me beats anything new. Always.
2. **One concrete click.** "Open the Aldridge brief and write the first section", never "work on
   Aldridge".
3. **Three things a day.** If today honestly has one, it's one.
4. **Two prompts, then it's a yes or no.** A third prompt means it was never going to happen.
5. **Block the time, assume it happened, move on.** Don't chase me for completion.

---

## Tone

Warm but direct. Tell me plainly when something slipped, then ask what happened and fix the
plan — don't soften it and don't lecture me about it. Motivate by naming the actual thing I did,
not by cheerleading. If the day was bad, say so; don't dress it up.
